# PAINDICATOR — Elevator Pitches (Poster Show)

Spoken scripts for the poster session. Three lengths so you can match the listener.

---

## 2-minute pitch (~300 words, ≈2:00)

Have you ever tried to describe *exactly* where it hurts? It's surprisingly hard. And yet that's how
pain is documented in clinics today — a patient points vaguely, or a doctor sketches on a paper body
chart. It's subjective, it's not measurable, and you can't compare it from one visit to the next.
That's a real problem, because managing pain is all about tracking change.

This is **PAINDICATOR**. Instead of paper, the patient paints their pain — its location *and* its
intensity — directly onto an interactive **3D model of the human body**, right on a tablet. Here's the
key idea: every single point on that model is mapped to a **dermatome** — the patch of skin served by
one spinal nerve. So the moment the patient paints, we automatically turn a subjective drawing into
**quantitative, anatomical data**.

The system then ranks which spinal nerve levels are most involved, measures how intense and how
widespread the pain is, even flags when two neighboring nerves are ambiguous — and it produces a clear,
**explainable** report that points the clinician toward the likely source and red-flags emergencies
like cauda equina. It's **decision support — not a diagnosis** — and it's transparent, not a black box.

We built it as our **biomedical engineering project at Tel Aviv University**, together with
**Loewenstein Rehabilitation Hospital** — so it solves a problem clinicians actually have. And it's
finished: it runs **offline on a tablet, in English and Hebrew**, with the full patient and clinician
workflow working today.

Our vision is to turn *"where does it hurt?"* into something you can finally **measure and track over
time**. Can I show you how the dermatome analysis works?

---

## 30-second version (~75 words)

PAINDICATOR turns pain documentation from a paper sketch into measurable data. A patient paints where
it hurts onto a 3D body on a tablet — and because every point maps to a spinal nerve's dermatome, we
instantly get quantitative, per-nerve pain metrics and an explainable clinical report that flags the
likely source and any red flags. We built it at Tel Aviv University with Loewenstein Hospital. It's
decision support — and it works today.

---

## One-liner

"We turn a patient's pain drawing into measurable, per-nerve clinical data on a 3D body."

## Delivery tips
- Pause after the opening question — let the gap land before you pitch.
- Point as you speak: to the 3D model on "paint," to the analysis on "ranks which nerve levels."
- Read the listener: open with the 30-second version; expand to 2 minutes only if they lean in.
- End with the question ("Can I show you…?") — the pitch starts a conversation, it doesn't finish one.
- Keep numbers ready for follow-ups (6 metrics · 31 dermatomes · offline EXE) — see context_pack.md §18.
- Rehearse aloud to ~2:00; if long, trim the third paragraph first.
- Honest framing always: decision support, not a diagnosis.
